# Phase 08.1: Version conflicts carry the standing version - Context

**Gathered:** 2026-10-07
**Status:** Ready for planning
**Mode:** Gap closure (inserted after the v1.1 audit; implementation decisions only, no product grey areas)

<domain>
## Phase Boundary

Closes a gap in MEM-02: a `version.*` conflict on a decision must be re-submitted with the right `expected_version`. Phase 8 does this by re-reading memory, and the re-read cannot find every decision.

- The swarm client (`swarm/memory.py`, `_current_version`) learns the version in force through `memory_search`, first with the subject as query text at the scope and then scope-wide.
- `memory_search` matches only the entry body: `to_tsvector('english', body)` in `packages/mcp-server/src/index-store.ts:1185`. It returns at most 100 entries, newest first. A subject query therefore misses a decision whose body lacks its subject words. The scope-wide read misses it once 100+ newer entries share the scope.
- The gate's conflict reply names the standing entry (`conflict_with`) but not its version. The client has no other way to learn the version.
- Phase 8's swarm tests did not catch this because the fake `memory_search` in `tests/test_swarm_memory.py` answered the subject query (`"db choice"`) with the decision whatever its body. The real server does not match on subject.
- Live reproduction on the merged mains: decision subject `db choice`, body `use postgres 16`, 120 newer facts at scope `repo:acme/scratch`. A rewrite without `expected_version` ended `status=conflict reason=version.required` after 1 POST and 2 searches. The decision stayed at v1.

In scope: the substrate reports the version in force on versioned conflicts, and the swarm client uses it. Out of scope: `memory_search` semantics (body-only matching and the 100-entry cap stay), other conflict reasons, and the rest of the gate.

</domain>

<decisions>
## Implementation Decisions

### Claude's Discretion
- **The server reports `current_version` on versioned conflicts. No subject filter is added to `memory_search`.** `MemoryWriteResult` gains `current_version?: number | null`. It is present exactly when the outcome is `conflict`, the reason is one of `version.required`, `version.stale`, `version.raced` or `standing.raced`, the write's conflict plan resolves `versioned` (a decision with a subject), and the lookup of the entry in force succeeded. The value is the `version` of the decision now in force for that scope and subject, or `null` when none is in force. It is absent on every other conflict and when the lookup failed. Reason: the gate already holds the standing entry when it decides the conflict. Reporting its version removes a round trip and the race window between the conflict and the re-read. A subject filter on search would still need that second read and would widen a public tool's contract.
- **The client keeps the re-read only as the fallback for older servers.** If the reply has `current_version` as an int (not a bool), the client resubmits with that `expected_version`. If it is `null`, the client resubmits without one. In both cases it makes no `memory_search` call. If the key is absent, `_current_version` runs exactly as it does today. `MAX_VERSION_RESUBMITS` and the same `idempotency_key` still apply. Reason: a newer client against an older server must not regress, and keeping the behaviour unchanged when the key is absent makes that easy to prove.
- **The test fake answers honestly.** The fake `memory_search` stops matching on subject. The fallback tests model body-only matching, and new tests drive the `current_version` path. Reason: the dishonest fake is how the gap passed phase 8. A fake that is more generous than the server proves nothing.
- **A real-gate Postgres regression and a live end-to-end run guard it.** A substrate test runs against `SUBSTRATE_TEST_PG_URL` with a decision behind 100+ newer entries in its scope and asserts that the conflict carries the right `current_version`. A live run rewrites such a decision through the swarm client. Reason: unit fakes missed this once. Only the real index proves the body-only search no longer matters.

</decisions>

## Specific Ideas

- Regression shape on both sides: decision subject `db choice`, body `use postgres 16`, 120 newer entries at the same `repo:` scope, rewrite without `expected_version`. Expected: one conflict carrying `current_version: 1`, then a resubmit that is accepted at v2 with v1 superseded, and no `memory_search` call.
