Hit predicate `expiry_stable(a, b)` — the only per-key hit test: both reads have exists=1 and pttl > 0, the call(s) between them started at least 1 s after read a, and |(b_read_ms + b_pttl) − (a_read_ms + a_pttl)| ≤ 250 ms, i.e. the key's absolute expiry did not move. A hit never writes; a miss re-SETs with a fresh TTL, which moves the absolute expiry by at least the 1 s gap; an expired or evicted key reads exists=0 (PTTL −2) and fails. Comparing raw PTTLs is not enough (a rewrite followed by a slow second read also shows a smaller PTTL).
Watcher (/var/lib/substrate/phase8-baseline/08-07-watch.sh, 0700 root, first line `#!/bin/bash`): loops every 5 s — `curl -s -m 4 http://127.0.0.1:7410/healthz | /usr/bin/jq -r '.cache.connected'` — and prints `<epoch seconds> connected=<value>` only when the value differs from the previous one (the first value is printed too); started as `sudo systemd-run --collect --unit=p8-healthz-watch -p RuntimeMaxSec=14400 /bin/bash /var/lib/substrate/phase8-baseline/08-07-watch.sh` (`--collect` unloads the unit even when the 4 h cap ends it as failed, so a re-run can reuse the name; its journal stays readable), read with `journalctl -u p8-healthz-watch -o cat`.
