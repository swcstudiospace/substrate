### Recall in the brief (uplift-4 S5, Build Plan BP-5.0)

The brief can carry cross-session recall from Hindsight as a `Recalled context` section. With `SUBSTRATE_BRIEF_RECALL=enforce` BP-5.0 ("Hindsight cross-session recall feeding `memory.brief`") is met; the default (`off`) changes nothing and sends no Hindsight call.

**Under `enforce` the brief reads the same fan-out `memory_recall` runs, from the cache when a fresh answer is held and live otherwise.** The banks are the caller's own bank plus the configured `sub-*` banks (`HINDSIGHT_BANKS`), one query per bank. `brief.ts`, `brief-profile.ts` and `recall-snapshot.ts` import nothing from `planes/hindsight` (a test in `memory-recall.test.ts` reads the three sources); the Hindsight calls reach them as injected functions. The fan-out's answer is one read of the class `recall-snapshot`, keyed by the memory and recall generations (see the bullet below); a failed read fails to "no section". **Recall is only for an authenticated caller whose bearer is the surface the brief names.** The brief's `surface` field (REST body, MCP argument) is attribution only; a bearer for another surface, and an anonymous caller (no bearer), get no recall: nothing is registered, read or composed for them, they are counted `disabled`, and they share the recall-off cache keys, so they never receive a brief written with another surface's recall. In-process `Substrate.brief()` callers have no bearer and get none either. Recall also needs the cache: with it disabled the section, the fan-out and the prefetch are all off and boot says so. The prefetcher fills the same class off the request path, for its own surface: a timer (`SUBSTRATE_BRIEF_RECALL_INTERVAL_S`, default 120, floor 30) and a debounced (5 s) hook after a live retain (`afterLiveRetain`).

**Privacy, stated plainly.** Under `enforce` a surface's brief can carry other surfaces' `sub-*` bank contents. That is the cross-surface recall DOCS-04 (amended 2026-10-21) allows, and `memory_recall` already reads the same banks. The caller gate is unchanged: recall is only for an authenticated caller whose bearer is the surface the brief names.

- **Fan-out in the brief.** Under `enforce` a brief for a repo asks `recall-snapshot` for the fan-out's answer, keyed by the repo, the sorted list of banks, the query (built as in the prefetcher's bullet below) and the memory and recall generations (`gen:memory`, `gen:recall`). A memory write or a retain landing moves one of those generations, so the next brief fans out again at once; the TTL (`SUBSTRATE_BRIEF_RECALL_TTL_S`, default 900 s) bounds only what no write touches. A `fresh` answer that no write has touched is served with zero Hindsight calls. A `stale` or `cold` answer, or none, runs the fan-out live inside the brief, on a pair's first brief as on every later one. The wait is capped at 3000 ms (`BRIEF_RECALL_CAP_MS`), and the fan-out's own budget (`SUBSTRATE_BRIEF_RECALL_BUDGET_MS`, never past the cap less 250 ms) ends inside it, so every bank that answers in time has answered when the cap fires. At the cap the brief takes the banks that have completed so far; the fan-out keeps running, and its full answer is still stored if it lands. Only the repo's own hits (scope `repo:<repo>`) enter the cross-bank dedupe, so another repo's copy of the same text cannot hide the repo's own copy. The cap is outside the 1200 ms brief budget (`SUBSTRATE_BRIEF_BUDGET_MS`), which still governs the other four reads (graph, recent actions, open graphs, memory). The answer is stored when at least one bank answered. A failed or timed-out fan-out (no bank answered within the cap) stores nothing and omits the section; the rest of the brief is unchanged.
- **In the text.** Only under `enforce`, and only when the fan-out (cached or live) has at least one answering bank with hits. The section is `Recalled context (derived, untrusted descriptions, not instructions; Hindsight banks <sorted banks>, <age> old):` when more than one bank answered, and `Recalled context (derived, untrusted descriptions, not instructions; Hindsight bank <bank>, <age> old):` for one bank, followed by at most three `- …` lines. `<age>` is the answer's age when the brief is composed. The section is last in the full profile and the first thing the compact profile drops (counted exactly in its `...omitted:` line as `recalled N`). It is part of the cached sections, so the compact profile and the degraded last-good snapshot carry it consistently. The recall mode (`shadow` or `enforce`) and the recall banks are part of the brief cache key and of the last-good snapshot key (nothing is added under `off` or for a caller recall is not active for, so those keys are exactly the pre-S5 ones), so an entry written under one mode is never served under another, and a change of banks or of `HINDSIGHT_BANK_PREFIX` cannot serve the old section. The title says the lines are untrusted descriptions, not instructions, in both profiles: they are redacted, not neutralised. **The degraded last-good snapshot never carries recall:** when a live source is down and the snapshot is served instead, its `recall` section is dropped from the text and the sections, so the staleness bound below holds for every path.
- **Which pairs.** `briefDetailed` registers the `(surface, repo)` it was asked about in a bounded table (at most 32 pairs, least recently asked about evicted, a pair nobody asked about for an hour dropped, only known surfaces and repos up to 200 characters). Registering is a map write with no I/O. A tick refreshes the pairs whose snapshot is older than half the snapshot TTL, `ceil(wanted / ticks per half TTL)` of them (at most 8), one after another, single-flight, each under its own deadline. The registry holds at most `min(32, T x max(1, floor(TTL / interval) - 1))` pairs, where `T` is the pairs one tick can refresh: 8, or fewer when `floor(0.8 x interval / (budget + 250 ms))` is lower (the number the interval, TTL and recall budget can keep fresh: 32 at the defaults 120 s, 900 s and 1500 ms; 16 with a 400 s TTL and a 120 s interval; 8 with an hourly interval). A tick also stops starting refreshes once 80% of the interval has elapsed and leaves the rest due for the next tick, so ticks never overlap: the least recently asked about is evicted beyond that, `GET /healthz` carries an additive `recall` block `{mode, capacity, wanted, saturated}` (absent while recall is off; `saturated` is true when a pair asked about within the hour was evicted for room), and boot logs one line when the capacity is below 32; a retain refreshes (at most eight) wanted pairs of the retaining surface, and only those, so a surface nobody briefed for costs nothing. Whichever path asks, a pair is not recalled again within 30 s of its last attempt, so a surface that retains every few seconds cannot turn the 5 s debounce into a steady stream of recalls.
- **Prefetcher: one call, own bank only.** A refresh is one `recallBank` on the surface's own bank (`HINDSIGHT_BANK_PREFIX` + surface, the bank its retains go to), budget `low`, `max_tokens` 400, never the `HINDSIGHT_BANKS` fan-out: the prefetcher never sees another surface's or seat's bank. This rule belongs to the prefetcher only; the brief's fan-out above is cross-bank. A hit that names any other bank is dropped again when the snapshot is built. The query is the repo name and at most three decision/warning subjects of the brief (80 characters each, redacted). Retains carry the scope in metadata, not as a tag, so hits are post-filtered to the exact scope `repo:<repo>`; the rest are dropped. The kept hits (at most 3, 240 characters each, single line, deduplicated) pass the shared redactor; the snapshot records the bank and the fetch time. The prefetcher's snapshot feeds only the `substrate.brief.recall` metric; the brief's section does not read it.
- **Stored.** The prefetcher's refresh does `cache.put` into `recall-snapshot`, keyed by the surface, the repo and the bank (the bank prefix is an operator setting that changes the answer), with an entry life of twice `SUBSTRATE_BRIEF_RECALL_TTL_S` (default 900, so 1800 s): the first half is `fresh`, the second `stale`. The fan-out's answer lives in the same class under its own key (repo, sorted banks, query, and the memory and recall generations), for `SUBSTRATE_BRIEF_RECALL_TTL_S` itself with no stale half (its `refreshAt`; the class limit is the setting's maximum, so the configured lifetime is honoured). A failed or empty prefetch refresh never replaces a good snapshot; it ages out. The deadline `SUBSTRATE_BRIEF_RECALL_BUDGET_MS` (default 1500) is the prefetch's abort signal, so it fires inside the breaker-gated fetch and a timeout counts as a plane failure. With the breaker open, Hindsight unconfigured or the cache unusable, nothing is sent and the attempt is counted (`skipped_breaker`, `skipped_unconfigured`, `skipped_cache`).

**Staleness bound.** A cached brief can carry a recall section up to the brief cache TTL plus the snapshot TTL (the freshness limit) old: 300 s + 900 s = 1200 s at the defaults (an estimate from those defaults, not a measurement). The `<age> old` in the title is the snapshot's age when the brief was composed, not when it is read.

**Shadow to enforce.**

1. Set `SUBSTRATE_BRIEF_RECALL=shadow` (an environment change and a restart of `substrate-mcp`; the code does neither). The prefetcher runs and every brief is counted in `substrate.brief.recall{surface,result}`; the served text, ETag and headers are unchanged.
2. Read `would_include` against `cold`, `stale` (a snapshot older than the TTL: the prefetcher is behind, so read `substrate.recall.prefetch` for `error` and `skipped_*`) and `disabled` for the surfaces that matter for a few days. `would_include` is the share of briefs that would have carried the section; `substrate.recall.prefetch{result}` says why the rest could not (`empty`, `error`, `skipped_*`). Check `brief.p95` has not moved (the brief adds one cache read, no plane call).
3. Read one snapshot by hand (a brief from a scratch process with `enforce`, or the cache key) and confirm the lines are what the configured banks hold for that repo.
4. Set `SUBSTRATE_BRIEF_RECALL=enforce` and restart. Briefs from `enforce` start with a cold cache of their own (the mode is in the key). A pair's first brief runs the fan-out itself, capped at 3000 ms, so `included` appears on the first brief; it no longer waits for the first prefetch.
5. Rollback: `SUBSTRATE_BRIEF_RECALL=off` and a restart. Snapshots are cache-only and expire; no flush is needed, and the pre-S5 cache keys stand again.

**Hindsight inventory (read-only, `SUBSTRATE_HINDSIGHT_INVENTORY=on`, default off).** `planes/hindsight.ts` gained three `GET` methods (`listBanks`, `bankStats`, `bankConfig`) with the closed op labels `bank-list`, `bank-stats` and the existing `bank-config`. A probe every 60 s (floor 30) reports per-bank gauges and a drift report (configured banks the deployment lacks, `sub-*` banks it has that nobody configured, banks whose retain extraction mode is not `chunks`) on `/healthz.hindsight`. The retain outbox gauges (`substrate.hindsight.retain_outbox.*`) are in-process and always on. Nothing is written to Hindsight by this item. Details and bounds: `docs/observability.md` § Hindsight outbox gauges, inventory and drift. Rollback: `SUBSTRATE_HINDSIGHT_INVENTORY=off`.

**Graph scope (v1.6 phase 57, `SUBSTRATE_BRIEF_RECALL_SCOPE=off|graph`, default `off`).** Under `graph`, and only with `SUBSTRATE_BRIEF_RECALL=enforce`, a brief that names a `graph_id` also prints up to three recall lines about that graph (`- [graph <id>] ...`) after the surface's own lines, from the same fan-out and under their own budget (the own-bank lines never spend it). A memory counts only when it was retained with that exact `graph_id` (the retain metadata carries it) and a bank that answered. The fan-out answer is cached per graph and the brief gets its own cache key part (`recallScope`), so a graph-scoped brief is never served as an unscoped one or as another graph's. `off` is byte for byte what the brief was: no key part, no extra line. A value other than `off` or `graph` is `off` and named at boot. Rollback: `off` and a restart; nothing is stored outside the cache.

The cache covers `POST /brief`, `memory_brief`, `memory_search`, `memory_recall` and `graph_get`. It does not cover writes, `events_*` or leases. Keys are `substrate:v1` plus a hash of the normalized arguments, the caller surface and a generation; the generation keys are read in one `MGET` before the entry is read. An accepted memory write, and a write whose commit is uncertain, bumps the memory generation. A `memory_search` also depends on the shared graphs generation, because a `ttl: graph` entry's liveness is read off its graph's status and a finished graph must drop the searches that still list it; and the expiry mode (`SUBSTRATE_MEMORY_EXPIRY=enforce`) is part of the memory-search, `brief` and `brief-snapshot` keys, so an entry cached under `report` is never served under `enforce`. A graph write — a claim, release, completion or registration — bumps that graph's generation and a shared graphs generation, so a brief that lists other graphs refreshes too. A heartbeat bumps only the graph's own generation: a renewed lease changes no text a repo brief shows before its refresh deadline, and bumping the shared key on every heartbeat is what held the hit ratio at 1 %. A stored answer expires at its class TTL or at the next lease or reverify deadline, whichever is sooner. A brief composed while any source failed or missed its budget is never stored. The `brief-snapshot` class is the exception to the generation rule: keyed by surface and arguments only, written when a brief is composed complete, read only when the live compose came back degraded, and served with a first line `_(substrate brief snapshot from <time>; live sources degraded: <sources>)_` — see `docs/observability.md` for the headers that mark it. `GET /healthz` adds `{enabled, connected, mode, hits, misses, bypasses, errors, cooldownUntil}` with `mode` of `off`, `on` or `degraded`. The status code does not depend on the cache. The same counters are the `substrate.cache.operations` metric.

Concurrent identical misses share one load (uplift-4 S4): the in-process in-flight map is keyed by the final cache key (so a caller that reads a newer generation has a different key and never joins an older flight), holds at most 256 entries (beyond that requests load independently), and an entry is deleted the moment its promise settles, however it settles. A caller that joins a flight whose owner produced a degraded answer (the brief's `store` refused it) loads for itself, because "degraded" belongs to the owner's call. A flight older than 15 s is not joined. A caller that passes its own budget (`deadlineMs`) and a degraded answer (`onDeadline`) to `getOrLoad` keeps that deadline while it waits on another caller's load: when its own timer wins it answers its degraded reply without starting a load and without cancelling the shared one, so a short-budget search never waits out a long-budget recall. This is always on and changes no answer, only the number of duplicate loads. `SUBSTRATE_CACHE_SWR=off|shadow|enforce` (default `shadow`) and `SUBSTRATE_CACHE_SWR_WINDOW_S` (default 30, max 300) add stale-while-revalidate for **the brief only**: a brief miss (a generation moved) that finds a `brief-snapshot` last-good younger than the window is answered with that text in `enforce`, while one background refresh per key (at most 16 at once) composes a fresh brief, stores it and moves the last-good. The snapshot records the lease or reverify deadline its text embeds (`until`, epoch ms, or `null` for none); a last-good past its `until`, one with an unreadable `until`, and one written by a release that did not record it at all (no `until`: its deadline is unknown) are never served stale. A degraded refresh is never stored and never becomes the last-good; a refresh that outlives its 15 s backstop deadline writes nothing when it finally settles and keeps its slot and its key until it does, so neither the 16-refresh cap nor one-refresh-per-key loosens. `shadow` takes the same decision beside the live load (no extra serial round trip), counts it as `would_serve_stale` and serves the live brief exactly as `off` does; the probe is started and never awaited, so a slow snapshot read cannot hold a finished brief or the flight slot, and it is silent, so a failing probe read neither counts an error nor marks the cache degraded. A stale brief keeps `state: complete` and is advertised additively: `POST /brief` adds `X-Substrate-Brief-Age: <seconds>` (on the 304 too) and the MCP `memory_brief` `structuredContent` adds `age_s` (also on `not_modified`); both are absent unless the brief was served stale. Read-your-writes therefore holds only in `off` and `shadow`: in `enforce` a brief read right after a write can be up to the window old until the refresh lands. `memory_search`, `memory_recall`, `graph_get` and `docs_search` are single-flight only: they have no generation-free last-good, and serving a pre-write memory or docs answer is not a trade this uplift makes. `substrate.cache.operations` carries `class` (`brief`, `memory-search`, `graph-view`, `brief-snapshot`, `docs-search`, `budget`, `gen`) and `result` (`hit|miss|coalesced|stale|refresh|would_serve_stale|bypass|error`) beside the existing `mode`. `bun scripts/cache-stats.ts` is the operator-run census of key classes, value bytes and the `maxmemory` they justify (bounded `SCAN`, `TYPE` and `STRLEN` only, every command raced against a 250 ms wall budget, no value read, loopback URLs only unless `--allow-remote`), with the running service's hit, stale and coalesced ratios read from its loopback `/healthz` (which needs the bearer: `SUBSTRATE_TOKEN` or `SUBSTRATE_TOKEN_FILE`, sent only as a header; a 401/403 is named in the output, not a silent gap).

### Endpoint policy

Each data-plane endpoint is classed before `substrate-mcp` connects to it. There are three classes:

- **loopback**: `localhost`, `127.x.y.z`, `::1` or `[::1]`.
- **proxy**: a host and port listed in `SUBSTRATE_DB_PROXY_ALLOW`.
- **direct**: anything else, including any URL that does not parse.

The scheme does not decide the class. When a URL names no port, the scheme's default port is used for the match: `http` 80, `https` 443, `postgres` and `postgresql` 5432, `redis` and `rediss` 6379. Host spellings are canonicalised before the match, so `127.1` is loopback. The address `0.0.0.0` is not loopback.

`SUBSTRATE_DB_PROXY_ALLOW` is a comma-separated list of `host:port` entries, for example a tailnet proxy listening on a chosen port (`proxy-host:5432`). Entries are trimmed, a host is lowercased, and the port must be 1 to 65535. A malformed entry refuses boot, naming the variable and the entry's position, never its text. Blank means no entries.

`SUBSTRATE_DB_ENDPOINT_POLICY` is `warn` (default) or `enforce`. Any other value refuses boot, naming the variable.

- `warn`: nothing is refused. A direct endpoint is reported at boot as a `db.endpoint` audit event.
- `enforce`: a configured direct endpoint refuses boot with a message naming the variable (`GREPTIME_URL`, `SUBSTRATE_PG_URL` or `DRAGONFLY_URL`) and the policy, never the URL.

DragonflyDB: a `redis` or `rediss` URL is accepted only when its class is loopback or proxy. Any other class is refused and the cache is off (fail-open), as before. The refusal of `rediss` to a non-loopback host is new.

Boot audit: one `db.endpoint` event (kind `note`, surface `substrate`, runtime `mcp-server`) is emitted per configured plane, once at listen, with a payload of `action`, `runtime`, `plane`, `class` and `policy`. It carries no URL, host or credential, and it never blocks boot.

Per-call access is metered by the existing plane duration and error metrics, not by per-call audit events, to avoid flooding the ledger.

Not done here and operator-gated: the Tailscale forwarders, their ACL, the allow list values, the cutover, and the live proof. RBAC and connection pooling are deferred.

### Request-path flags (uplift-5 U5-3)

Three flags, each defaulting to what the code did before them. An unrecognised value keeps the default and is named on stderr at boot (`misconfigured — …`); nothing here deletes data or changes a key shape under the default.

**`SUBSTRATE_BRIEF_GRAPH_READ=full|lite` (default `full`).** `full` reads a graph-scoped brief's graph with `getGraph`: four statements one after another (`graph_index`, `graph_nodes`, `graph_steps`, `graph_edges`). `lite` reads it with `getGraphForBrief`: the `graph_index` row and the `graph_nodes` rows, issued together (`Promise.allSettled`), with `steps` and `edges` left empty. The brief prints the graph's status, surface and PR link and its nodes, and neither steps nor edges, so the text, the refresh deadline and the failed-source list are the same (a test renders no nodes, many nodes, held and lapsed leases, a PR link and a missing graph both ways and compares them). A failed index read, or a failed node read under an existing row, is still the `graph` failed source; a node read that fails for a graph with no row is a missing graph, as it is under `full`. Because it waits on both statements (`Promise.allSettled`), a hung `graph_nodes` statement delays surfacing a `graph_index` failure, and that failure's plane and breaker sample, until the brief deadline, where the serial `full` read would have failed at once; the brief text and the failed-source result are unchanged. Only the brief's source read uses it: `graph_get`, claims and every other caller keep `getGraph`. Verify on `/metrics`: the plane histogram `substrate.plane.duration` for `plane=index` carries `op=graph.brief` under `lite` and `op=graph.get` under `full`.

**`SUBSTRATE_CACHE_BUMP_SCOPE=legacy|narrow` (default `legacy`).** `legacy` is the behaviour above: an accepted write bumps the memory generation, a successful Hindsight retain bumps it again, and so does an outbox replay, so one forwarded write flushes every brief and every memory search twice; a heartbeat bumps the graph's own key, which a graph-scoped brief lists. `narrow` changes two things and nothing else:

1. The post-retain and outbox-replay bumps move from the memory generation to a new `gen:recall` counter. The accept bump stays on `gen:memory`. One accepted write that is retained therefore flushes briefs once, not twice.
2. A graph-scoped brief stops listing the graph's own key. A heartbeat bumps only that key, so a heartbeat no longer flushes briefs. `bumpGraph` (register, create, claim, release, complete, reap) and the reaper still bump the graph key **and** `gen:graphs`, and the brief still lists `gen:graphs`, so every write that changed text a brief prints still flushes it. A renewed claim that skips its event still goes through `bumpGraph` and still flushes, as today.

What each entry class depends on, and what covers each input (the same list `legacy` uses, with the `narrow` differences marked):

| Class (cache key class) | Inputs that change the answer | Covered by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Brief (`brief`, and its generation-free `brief-snapshot`) | repo, branch, graph id, expiry mode, recall mode and bank, surface | repo, branch, graph id, expiry mode, recall mode and bank and surface are in the key. Memory writes: `gen:memory`. Any graph write that changes a node, status, surface or PR link: `gen:graphs` (every `bumpGraph` and the reaper bump it). The graph's own key was only ever bumped additionally by a heartbeat, which changes no text: a node line prints the claim time, never the lease expiry, and `refreshAt` already ends an entry at the earliest lease expiry baked into it. `legacy` also lists the graph key; `narrow` does not. The recall section is read from the generation-free `recall-snapshot` class, so neither `gen:memory` nor `gen:recall` ever covered it: a retain moves nothing the brief shows, which is why the retain bump is not needed here. |
| Paged memory search (`memory-search`, `memory_search` with a cursor or subject prefix) | query, scope, cursor, limit, superseded, expiry mode, surface, repo; the index rows; a `ttl: graph` entry's graph status | Key: the arguments, expiry mode, surface, repo. `gen:memory` (every accepted write), `gen:graphs` (graph status). Reads the index only, so it does not list `gen:recall`. |
| Merged memory search (`memory-search`, `memory_search`) | everything above, plus the Hindsight recall merged into it | As above, plus `gen:recall` under `narrow` (the retain landing is what changes Hindsight). Under `legacy` the retain bump is `gen:memory`, which it already lists. |
| Recall search (`memory-search`, `memory_recall`) | query, banks, surface, repo (optional for `memory_recall` and `POST /memory/recall`; null when the caller names none); what Hindsight holds | Key: query, banks, surface, repo. `gen:memory` (an accepted write, before its retain lands) and, under `narrow`, `gen:recall` (the retain or its replay landing). It lists no graph key: a recall does not read the graph. |
| Graph view (`graph-view`, `graph_get`) | graph id, surface; every node and lease column, including lease expiry | The graph's own key, which every `bumpGraph` and every heartbeat bumps, under both scopes. A heartbeat is the one write that changes the lease expiry a view prints, which is why the view keeps the heartbeat bump and only the brief drops it. `refreshAt` ends an entry at the earliest lease expiry in it. |

Staleness: under `narrow` a recall cached after an accept and before its retain lands is flushed when the retain lands (the recall and merged-search entries list `gen:recall`); a brief cached in that gap is not flushed by the retain under `narrow` unless it is under `enforce`, and does not need to be for index-derived text. Under `SUBSTRATE_BRIEF_RECALL=enforce` a brief does embed a Hindsight-derived `Recalled context` section. It is read from the `recall-snapshot` fan-out answer, whose key carries the memory and recall generations, so a memory write or a retain landing refreshes it at once under either scope; under `enforce` the brief's own entry lists `gen:recall` as well, so a retain under `narrow` refreshes the brief text too. The TTL (`SUBSTRATE_BRIEF_RECALL_TTL_S`, default 900 s) bounds only what no write touches: a fresh fan-out answer that no write has touched is served until it ages past the TTL, and a stale or cold one is fanned out live again (bounds: § Recall in the brief). After a heartbeat a cached brief and a recomposed one carry the same facts; relative times in the text (`held by X 10 min ago`) drift with the age of the cached entry under both scopes, as they do today. There is no new TTL and no new key class; `gen:recall` is one more counter in the existing `gen` class. Rollback: unset the variable and restart `substrate-mcp`; the counter is bumped only under `narrow`, so under `legacy` it stays unset and the keys that list it do not move.

**`/healthz` single flight and `SUBSTRATE_HEALTHZ_INDEX_CAPACITY_MEMO_S` (default `0`).** While `SUBSTRATE_HEALTHZ_CACHE_MS` is above 0, probes that arrive while a computation is running join it instead of starting their own: a burst inside the window makes one `substrate.health()` call, one index ping and one capacity read, and every joiner answers with the same body (`stale: false`, since each waited for the computation). The shared promise is dropped in a `finally`, so a computation that throws fails its joiners (500, as before) and the next probe starts afresh. With `SUBSTRATE_HEALTHZ_CACHE_MS=0` every call computes, as before. `SUBSTRATE_HEALTHZ_INDEX_CAPACITY_MEMO_S` reuses the index size report (`capacity.index`) for that many seconds, at most 300 (a larger value is capped and named at boot, a negative or non-numeric one is `0` and named): `0` measures on every computed body, as before. A measurement still running past the one-second probe bound is joined rather than repeated, a failed or timed-out measurement stores nothing (the last good report stays until it ages out, then the field is `null` again) and the capacity-runway sample is taken once per measurement. The capacity statement now computes `pg_total_relation_size` once per relation. The Greptime prelude and the index ping stay serial, as before: running them together would change which plane's failure is reported first and the order of the breaker samples.

Admission budgets (uplift-3 N3) are the one key class that is not a cached answer. `substrate:v1:budget:<surface>:<class>` is a hash `{t, at}` — the tokens left and the epoch ms of the last step — written by one `EVAL` (`BUDGET_SCRIPT` in `packages/mcp-server/src/admission.ts`) that refills at the class rate, checks, debits and `PEXPIRE`s the key to the burst's refill time (≥ 1 s), so an idle surface costs no key and a counter never outlives the state it carries. The identity is the bearer's surface, never a body-claimed one; accepted untokened bridge callers share `anonymous`. The call goes through the cache's plane gate like every other command (`op=eval`), so a timeout, an open breaker or the connect cooldown admits the caller and counts a `bypass` rather than refusing: the budgets bound load on the planes and must not make the cache a way to fail them. `SUBSTRATE_BUDGETS=off|shadow|enforce` (default `shadow`: decide and count, never refuse) and `SUBSTRATE_BUDGET_<CLASS>_RPS` / `_BURST` with per-surface `SUBSTRATE_BUDGET_<SURFACE>_<CLASS>_*` overrides are in `.env.example`; `docs/observability.md` has the metric and the `/slo` row, `docs/grok-bot.md` what a refused caller sees.

### Cache preload

`SUBSTRATE_CACHE_PRELOAD` is `on` or `off`, default `off`. `SUBSTRATE_CACHE_PRELOAD_TOP`
is an integer from 1 to 20, default 3. `SUBSTRATE_CACHE_PRELOAD_S` is an integer from
10 to 3600, default 300. An out-of-range value, or any other switch value, refuses boot
and names the variable. `on` with no cache because `DRAGONFLY_URL` is unset or
`SUBSTRATE_CACHE=0` refuses boot too, and names `SUBSTRATE_CACHE_PRELOAD`: a warm-up
with nowhere to store would only compose.

A `DRAGONFLY_URL` refused by the endpoint policy is a different case. Under `warn` the refusal
is fail-open, as for the cache: the server boots uncached with the preload off, and stderr logs
`SUBSTRATE_CACHE_PRELOAD=on skipped: the cache endpoint is refused, so the cache is off`. Under
`enforce` the refused endpoint refuses boot first (see Endpoint policy), so the preload's own
check is never reached.

With the switch on, the substrate counts brief requests per repo over a sliding hour,
for at most 256 repos, and remembers the last brief request seen for each repo: its
branch, graph_id and surface, the fields its cache key is built from. The least
recently seen repos are evicted at the cap.

Every interval the preload composes the brief for the top K repos (K is
`SUBSTRATE_CACHE_PRELOAD_TOP`), each as the last request named for it, through the
uncounted brief path, so its own warm-up never raises a repo's count. A warm-up refreshes
its entry: it composes and stores over a fresh entry, so the entry is replaced before it
expires. A warm-up that composes degraded or as a snapshot is counted as a failure and
stores nothing. It writes through the existing brief cache: there is no new cache class
and no new key scheme.

The surface stands as its own bearer during the warm-up, so the warmed keys are the keys
a real request reads. The consequence is that the preload performs that surface's recall
reads for its own bank, at most K per interval, on the schedule above. Any authenticated
caller can name a surface in a request, so this is a bounded amplification of traffic the
caller could already send.

It runs as one unref'd interval, started by the recall start path while the switch is on
and stopped at shutdown. A tick is skipped while the previous tick is still running. A
failed warm-up is counted and logged once per tick and is never thrown.

### Event wake-up hints

`SUBSTRATE_EVENT_BUS` is `on` or `off`; the default is `off`. Any other value refuses boot and names the variable. The bus uses the cache endpoint (`DRAGONFLY_URL`). When that URL is unset or refused by the endpoint policy, the bus is off and a reason is logged once at boot. The bus is also off when `SUBSTRATE_CACHE` is `0`.

With the bus on, the channel is `substrate:v1:events`. A message is a hint with exactly these fields: `v` (always `1`), `id`, `kind`, `surface`, `graph_id` and `ts`. It carries no summary, no payload and no text. A message over 1024 bytes is dropped and counted, never truncated. A received message that does not parse is ignored and counted.

Publish happens after the ledger write succeeds, fire-and-forget. A publish failure is counted and logged once per failure run; the emit still succeeds. The ledger is the source of truth.

`/events/stream` subscribes for the life of the response and unsubscribes when it closes. A hint wakes the stream's idle wait, so the stream reads the ledger at once. A missed hint costs latency, not data, because the stream still polls.

A dropped subscription reconnects with a backoff of 1 second, doubling to a maximum of 30 seconds. A successful reconnect resets the backoff.

Cache invalidation is not driven by hints. Generation counters remain the only invalidation authority, shared through Dragonfly. Dragonfly holds no authoritative state; the pub/sub channel carries only wake-up hints.

## Railway control plane (Phase 45)

Railway services are managed by the `railway_*` MCP tools. The control plane is the Railway public GraphQL API, not the forwarders (D45-a). Control calls go to the public API over HTTPS with a bearer token read from `RAILWAY_API_TOKEN` by name; the value is never logged, stored, put in an event or echoed in an error. The forwarders in [railway-tailscale.md](railway-tailscale.md) carry database and service traffic only, and their tailnet ACL does not name the Railway API, so the control path does not use them.

- **Reads need no approval (D45-b).** `railway_list`, `railway_monitor`, `railway_scale_suggest` and `railway_cost` make no change. `railway_scale_suggest` returns an advisory recommendation from the p95 of recent CPU and memory usage, with headroom, clamped to the configured bounds. It never applies a change, and it reports `insufficient-data` rather than guess when there are fewer than 24 samples.
- **Writes are refused by default (D45-c).** `railway_scale` (vCPU and memory limits) and `railway_deploy` (redeploy) act only when all three hold: `SUBSTRATE_RAILWAY_WRITES` is exactly `on`; the caller is an operator; and the call carries an `approval_ref` of letters, digits and `._:/#-`, at most 128 characters. Any failure refuses before any network request, with `writes-disabled`, `operator-required`, `approval-ref-missing`, `unconfigured` or `out-of-bounds`. Each write outcome is one event (`railway.scale` or `railway.deploy`): a `note` when applied, a `warning` when refused. Replica counts are not changed by this phase (D45-k).
- **The cost report is estimated usage times operator prices (D45-d).** It sums `estimatedUsage` for CPU, memory, disk and network transmit over `SUBSTRATE_RAILWAY_PROJECT_IDS`, and multiplies each by its `SUBSTRATE_RAILWAY_PRICE_*_USD` unit price. A price is never assumed: a line with usage and no price makes the status `unpriced`, and its raw value still shows. The projected monthly total is compared with `SUBSTRATE_RAILWAY_BUDGET_USD`, which defaults to 50, the owner's ceiling. A total above it is `over-budget`. The period and unit of the usage estimate are confirmed on the first live read, before any price is set.
- **The `/slo` cost row is report-only.** It is the `railwayCost` key, is never folded into `breached`, and is cached for 15 minutes. See [observability.md](observability.md).

The live write proof and the first live cost read are operator-gated, and each is recorded with its approval reference. Nothing in the automated tests reaches Railway.

## Unified resource fabric (Phase 51)

The `fabric_status`, `fabric_optimize` and `fabric_compliance` MCP tools give one read-only view of eight resources: Dragonfly, the TimescaleDB index, GreptimeDB, LightRAG, Hindsight, TEI (the embedding service, phase 64), the Tailscale proxy layer and the corpus (the docs plane's freshness, ingest throughput and golden-set recall against their objectives, see [`corpus-eval.md`](corpus-eval.md)). They read the same bounded health probes `/healthz` uses (shared within a short window), the Railway cost report and the Hindsight retain outbox, and add no probe of their own, except the `tei` row's one bounded `GET /health` and `GET /info` pair, which `/healthz` never makes. The `lightrag` row reads the LightRAG `GET /health` probe, its circuit-breaker state and whether `LIGHTRAG_URL` is configured; it is `unconfigured` when LightRAG is not configured and never `ok` without a true probe answer. The LightRAG row carries no endpoint class (`not-applicable`). The `tei` row is `unconfigured` without `TEI_URL`; configured, it carries the endpoint class of `TEI_URL` (loopback, proxy or direct), its breaker state and a `detail` of the model, dimension and last latency the client has seen (never the URL or the key), and it is `ok` only when `/health` answered and `/info` named the pinned model. The `fabric_compliance` check `tei-endpoint-private` fails a direct TEI URL.

- **Endpoint class.** The Tailscale dimension is the phase-48 class of each data-plane endpoint: `loopback`, `proxy` or `direct`. It is derived from `SUBSTRATE_DB_PROXY_ALLOW` and the data-plane URL variables (`GREPTIME_URL`, `SUBSTRATE_PG_URL`, `DRAGONFLY_URL`), and never printed. There is no separate Tailscale probe.
- **State.** Each row is `ok`, `degraded`, `unavailable` or `unconfigured`. A resource nothing configures is `unconfigured`, never `ok`. A configured resource that gives no answer within 1.5 seconds is `unavailable`, and its row names the reason.
- **Suggestions.** `fabric_optimize` lists deterministic suggestions, each with the evidence it fired on. It executes nothing and changes no configuration. Per-service scaling stays with `railway_scale_suggest`.
- **Compliance.** `fabric_compliance` reports PASS, FAIL or NOT_APPLICABLE per check. The endpoint checks read `SUBSTRATE_DB_ENDPOINT_POLICY`. An unconfigured resource is NOT_APPLICABLE, never PASS.
- **Environment.** The fabric adds no environment variable. It reads only names documented elsewhere in this file: `SUBSTRATE_DB_ENDPOINT_POLICY`, `SUBSTRATE_DB_PROXY_ALLOW`, `SUBSTRATE_CACHE`, `SUBSTRATE_EVENT_BUS` and the `SUBSTRATE_RAILWAY_*` budget and price names.

Limitation: the cache client has no ping, so a configured Dragonfly reads `unavailable` until a cache command has connected, and the fabric sends no command of its own to probe it.

## Embeddings: TEI (phase 64)

Substrate embeds text through the operator's TEI (text-embeddings-inference) service, `planes/tei.ts`, reached only from `substrate-mcp`. `POST /embed` takes at most `SUBSTRATE_TEI_BATCH` texts (32) and no more than the server's own `max_client_batch_size`, `GET /info` names the model, and `GET /health` says whether it is ready. Every call answers a value that never rejects, `{ ok: true, vectors, model, dim }` or `{ ok: false, reason }`, with a closed reason: `not-configured`, `breaker-open`, `timeout`, `unavailable`, `bad-response`, `model-mismatch`, `dim-mismatch` or `input-refused`. Vectors of two models are not comparable, so a TEI whose `/info` names a model other than `SUBSTRATE_TEI_MODEL`, or that answers a vector of a length other than `SUBSTRATE_TEI_DIM`, is refused and no vector is returned. A pin left unset is learned from the first good answer and held. With `TEI_URL` unset nothing is sent and every call answers `not-configured`.

- **Cache.** With `SUBSTRATE_EMBED_CACHE=on` a vector is cached under the class `emb`, key `substrate:v1:emb:<sha256 of model + "\n" + text>`, always with the TTL `SUBSTRATE_EMBED_CACHE_TTL_S` (604800 s). The class adds no redis command: reads are one `MGET`, writes are `SET ... EX`. Production's Dragonfly is a loopback redis with a 256 MB `volatile-lru` limit, so an entry is the vector's Float32 bytes in base64 and nothing else (about 2 KB for 384 dimensions).
- **Fabric.** `tei` is the eighth fabric resource; see the Unified resource fabric section above and [embedding-fabric](embedding-fabric.md).
- **Endpoint.** `TEI_URL` is classed like a data-plane endpoint (loopback, proxy or direct). Under `SUBSTRATE_DB_ENDPOINT_POLICY=enforce` a direct one refuses boot; under `warn` the fabric compliance check `tei-endpoint-private` fails it.

## Planning plane (phase 52)

The `planning_status` and `planning_sessions` MCP tools read the planning checkout named by `SUBSTRATE_PLANNING_ROOT`. Both are read-only: they write nothing on every call, not even a heartbeat, and both are fail-open in the audit.

- **Disabled.** With no root both answer `{ "state": "disabled" }`, never an error. Invalid settings answer `misconfigured` and name the keys, never the values.
- **Index.** `planning_status` reports counts, sha256 hashes, byte sizes and error records for the documents under `.planning/`. It never returns a file body or a frontmatter value. The index is keyed by content hash: identical bytes at any paths share one content entry, which lists the paths holding them. The walk is bounded by `SUBSTRATE_PLANNING_MAX_FILES` and `SUBSTRATE_PLANNING_MAX_BYTES`, and sets `truncated` when it stops early. An incremental update re-reads and re-hashes only the documents whose stat fingerprint changed; for the same tree it equals a full rebuild.
- **Observer.** The server's planning observer is the plane's only writer. While the plane is enabled it scans on a timer whose period is `SUBSTRATE_PLANNING_SCAN_INTERVAL_MS` (default 15000 ms, 1000..3600000), started at boot, unref'd and cleared at shutdown. A tick that arrives during a scan shares that scan; no second scan starts behind it. Shutdown waits for a scan in flight, inside its bound, and then flushes the baseline. A read never runs the observer's scan and never writes. `planning_status` does its own full walk and hash of the documents on every call, with no cache, so it is not a cheap poll. Each scan diffs the index against the baseline the process holds. A complete change is published once as one `planning.changed` note (surface `substrate`, no new kind) through the ledger and the bus hint. Each changed path carries its sha256 before and after (`{ path, before, after }`), never its text. With no durable baseline (see Baseline below) the first scan after a restart is a baseline and publishes nothing. A failed publish holds the baseline, so the same change is published on the next scan. The repo is the `remote.origin.url` of the checkout, asked of `git config --get` run in it (3 s bound, a fixed argv and an allowlisted environment), so an origin defined in an included config file (`include.path`, `includeIf`) is found as git finds it. Only when git cannot run at all is the origin read from the common git directory's `config` instead: a `.git` file (linked worktree) is followed through its `gitdir:` line, and `commondir` is followed when present; an inline `#` or `;` comment after the URL is not part of it. Git running but not answering (an error exit, a signal or the timeout) is a transient failure. A change with no repo is reported as `repo-unknown` with a `repoReason` and nothing is published. A transient failure to read the git data (denied, I/O, removed between the stat and the read, or git not answering) holds the baseline, so the next scan publishes the change once. A definitive absence advances the baseline. A failing scan, a failing heartbeat and a change the ledger did not store are reported on stderr when they appear or change.
- **Presence.** The observer heartbeats a checkout when it sees a complete change in it, and, while the plane is enabled, a scan that sees no change writes a liveness record: it renews the session's key and its liveness time without moving the time of its last planning change. `SUBSTRATE_PLANNING_LIVENESS_INTERVAL_MS` (default 0, every scan; 0..3600000) is the least time between those writes, and it never exceeds a third of the freshness window. The liveness beat also runs on its own timer, whose period is that interval (for 0, the scan interval) capped at a third of the freshness window, so a `SUBSTRATE_PLANNING_SCAN_INTERVAL_MS` longer than the freshness window cannot make a live session look dead; the timer beats for the checkout the last scan read and stops beating after a scan that could not read it. A session whose observer keeps running therefore stays `active` while it is idle for hours, and each `planning_sessions` row carries `idleForMs`, the time since its last planning change (null when the server clock cannot be read). The session id is `wt-` and the first 24 hex digits of the sha256 of the worktree path; the branch is read from the worktree's HEAD. Every heartbeat is stamped with the server's own `TIME`. A key is written with `SET ... EX` for the freshness window (`SUBSTRATE_PLANNING_PRESENCE_TTL_S`) plus a margin derived from it: the TTL plus clamp(2 x TTL, 300 s, 1 day), so a key lives at most 2 days. A session that goes stale stays listed as `stale` while its key is held. Staleness is judged against the server's time from the freshness window; the code never deletes a key. Enumeration is a bounded `SCAN` of at most 256 sessions; a larger set is marked `truncated`. When the server clock cannot be read, sessions are listed as `unknown`.
- **Residual risk.** The key expiry is server-side: Dragonfly removes a session key once its lifetime (the window plus the derived margin, at most 2 days) closes with no heartbeat of either kind. That is retention, not a code deletion, and a session whose observer was silent for longer than that drops out of `planning_sessions`. Presence stays ephemeral and cache-only: the observer baseline is never kept there.
- **Baseline.** With `SUBSTRATE_PLANNING_BASELINE_DIR` set to an absolute directory, the observer saves its publish baseline to a file there (written, synced and renamed, throttled, never on a scan's critical path) and loads it on the first scan after a restart, so a planning change made while the process was down is published once instead of being absorbed as a baseline. Unset keeps the baseline in memory. A baseline that cannot be read or is invalid is reported and the first scan is a baseline.
- **Position.** `planning_status` also carries `position`, the GSD-Core position parsed from `STATE.md` and `ROADMAP.md` (milestone, phase, plan, step, status and progress, redacted and capped). A missing or malformed file is an entry in the position's `errors`, never a failed call.
- **Commands.** The presence client sends only `GET`, `SET ... EX`, `SCAN` and `TIME`. Every reply has a deadline (`COMMAND_TIMEOUT_MS`, 250 ms, the cache's budget); a reply that misses it fails the call and the connection is discarded. It is a separate module from the cache client, so the cache's command lock is unchanged.
- **History.** Presence history is not yet written to TimescaleDB. That table is an operator-gated production step (see `.planning/phases/52-planning-awareness-core/PLAN.md`).

## Steering inbox (phase 53)

The `steering_queue`, `steering_acknowledge` and `steering_list` MCP tools carry directives from an operator to a planning session. They are registered while `SUBSTRATE_PLANNING_STEERING` is on, and also when it is malformed, so a bad setting is answered rather than hidden.

- **Queue.** The author is the calling operator's surface and is always human; it must be on `SUBSTRATE_PLANNING_STEERING_PRINCIPALS`. The server assigns the directive id (a random UUID), and a caller that supplies an `id` is refused as an unknown field. A non-operator or agent caller, an unlisted principal and a session past `SUBSTRATE_PLANNING_STEERING_RATE_PER_MIN` are refused with a typed error. A directive is audited before it is queued.
- **Acknowledge.** An operator, not the target agent, records `applied` or `declined`. Applying a conflicting directive requires `confirm: true`. Audit precedes effect. Unanswered directives expire as `declined`/`timeout` after `SUBSTRATE_PLANNING_STEERING_ACK_TTL_S` (default 86400, 60..2592000), through one bounded sweep every `SUBSTRATE_PLANNING_STEERING_EXPIRY_SWEEP_S` (default 60, 1..3600) while durable inbox auditing is configured.
- **Session state.** Applied pause maps to `PAUSED`, applied redirect to `CONTEXT_REFRESH`; annotate/declined/timeout change nothing. The authority-backed state port applies the legal transition in SQL. The reply reports applied/unchanged/refused. An accepted audited acknowledgement stands if close/outage/illegal transition prevents state application; no hint is fabricated. Only an actual authorized movement produces `checkpoint_hint`.
- **Delivery.** Private `docs_search` augmentation requires explicit `steering_session_id` plus owner binding proof or trusted operator read override; historical `session_id` never authorizes it. A notice contains the count and at most 3 private entries, with body snippets bounded to 200 characters. Private results/context are request-local, excluded from shared docs/memory caches and retrieval telemetry. Authorization/authority failures refuse rather than silently omitting private material. A `steering.queued` public note carries only directive/session identity and `non_blocking`, no command action/priority/body/count; optional bus hints/presence cannot authorize access.
- **List.** `steering_list` is a targeted private read, not discovery. It returns queued/acknowledged directives and checked state after final SQL authorization. It creates no inbox transition audit row; generic read audit policy remains separate.
- **Authorship.** The author comes only from the operator caller. No tool argument names an author, and the argument schemas refuse one as an unknown field.
- **Settings.** Invalid `SUBSTRATE_PLANNING_STEERING*` values make every steering tool answer `misconfigured`, naming the failing keys and never their values. Startup logs those keys once.
- **Sink.** Writes are fail-closed. With no audit sink they answer `not-configured` and record nothing. The durable sink is the Postgres table `planning_steering_audit`, created only when `SUBSTRATE_PLANNING_STEERING_AUDIT_TABLE` is on, after migrations 33 and 34 (the nullable `reason` column) are rehearsed on a restored dump; with it on and the index configured, the server builds the sink from it, so the steering writes work. A repeat of the same payload is a no-op. A different payload under a saved `(directive, transition)` key is an `AUDIT_CONFLICT`, and the write is refused. The table is append-only at the application level; a database-level REVOKE or trigger is an operator decision.
- **Restart.** The audit table is also the durable directive store: there is no second table. At boot, with the opt-in on, the server reads back the open directives (queued with no acknowledged row, at most 1000 per read) and loads them into the inbox before it answers; a restore writes no audit row. A read that fails is reported on stderr and the inbox starts empty; the rows stay in the table and boot is never refused. Without the opt-in nothing is read and nothing changes.

## Corpus manifest (phase 63)

The corpus atlas keeps one Postgres table, `corpus_documents` (migration step 35, additive, `expand`), in the index. It is the server's record of which version of each documentation file the docs plane holds, and nothing else reads or writes it.

- **Key and columns.** One row per `(repo, path)`: `kind`, `tier`, `sha256`, `bytes`, `source`, `commit_sha`, `state` (`active` or `tombstoned`), `docs_document_id` (the LightRAG document the active content lives in), `signals` (reserved, empty), `superseded_by` (migration step 38: the explicit `superseded-by` marker, a repo-relative path, or null), `manifest_seq`, and the timestamps. Indexes on `(repo, state)`, `sha256` and `docs_document_id`. Rows are never deleted: a removal is a `tombstoned` state.
- **Version.** `manifest_seq` comes from the table's own identity sequence, and every write that changes a row takes the next value, so the maximum (`manifestVersion` in a plan or status reply) only grows.
- **Writers.** `substrate-mcp` only: `POST /corpus/ingest` and the `corpus_ingest` tool, after the plane accepted a document (and, for an `update`, with no plane write: the row's `source`, `kind`, `tier` and `commit` are refreshed when the sha256 is equal). One request runs at a time per process, in the phases tombstones, inserts, superseded deletes (`docs/runbooks/corpus-ingest.md`). `POST /corpus/plan`, `GET /corpus/status` and `corpus_status` read it. All of them answer `503 corpus.disabled` while `SUBSTRATE_CORPUS` is off (the default), so shipping a release that holds the table exposes nothing.
- **Plane.** The ingest reaches LightRAG through the bulk plane (`planes/lightrag-bulk.ts`, `POST /documents/texts`) and no other way: the CLI sends documents to the server and never to LightRAG. Operations are in `docs/runbooks/corpus-ingest.md`; the dataset and its citations are in `docs/docs-plane.md`.
## Live steering GA (phase 67)

REST steering routes require a surface bearer (anonymous is 401) and are absent while `SUBSTRATE_PLANNING_STEERING` is off. Lifecycle MCP tools `docs_steering_session`, `steering_session_heartbeat` and `steering_session_close` register independently. Owner lifecycle mutations are not operator-only; directive queue/ack remain operator-only. REST uses snake_case and ISO timestamps, strict bodies, and proof only through `X-Substrate-Session-Binding`. MCP uses optional `session_binding`.

| Route | Tool | Class | Contract |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `POST /steering/sessions` | `docs_steering_session`, no ID | write | `{repo_id,state,steering?,planning_hash?}`; committed receipt has `status: issued`, public session/repo/state/updated/expiry fields, `event_stored` and one-time proof, never canonical private text/hash. No caller-selected ID/owner. State updates exist only through MCP with active ID/proof, returning `updated` and the same public receipt without proof. |
| `POST /steering/sessions/heartbeat` | `steering_session_heartbeat` | write | `{session_id}`; owner proof required even for operator; `renewed`, expiry. |
| `POST /steering/sessions/close` | `steering_session_close` | write | `{session_id}`; owner proof or operator override; committed terminal `closed`, `session_id`, `closed_at`, `event_stored`. |
| `GET /steering/sessions?repo_id=&limit=&cursor=` | discovery, not `steering_list` | read | SQL-filtered active metadata before cap; own surface, or all for operators. Limit 1..100/default 50; ascending public-handle keyset, `truncated`, optional `next_cursor`. No private text/hash/body/count. |
| `GET /steering/directives?session_id=&status=` | `steering_list` | read | proof-bound target or operator override; `queued`, `acknowledged`, `all`; `history`, `history_truncated`; hint only for an actual movement observed by this process. |
| `POST /steering/queue` | `steering_queue` | write | unchanged `{session_id,action,body,priority?,conflicts_with_active_plan?}`, operator policy; no new key/proof requirement. |
| `POST /steering/acknowledge` | `steering_acknowledge` | write | unchanged `{directive_id,outcome,confirm?}`; target inference after principal admission. MCP also requires matching `session_id`. |
| `GET /steering/wait?session_id=&after=&timeout_ms=` | no wait tool | poll: its own slot (32 held at once, `LONG_POLL_SLOTS_MAX`), no shared token | checked before hold and every release; 200 directive/`wait_ms`, or 204/`X-Steering-Wait-Ms`; slot-full 429 `{error:"budget",class:"poll"}` and waiter-cap 429 both `Retry-After: 1`. |
| `GET /session/context?repo_id=&session_id=&limit=` | `session_context` | read | public-redacted digest, no proof; flag-off 503 `session-context.disabled`. |

- **`session_context`.** A bounded, redacted digest of the other live sessions of one repository: `sessions`, `claims`, `events`, `handoffs` and `planning`, each item with its `source` and `age_s`, newest first, 24,000 bytes at most, `truncated` naming each list that lost items and `notes` naming each source that was not configured or did not answer. `truncated` records every cut, not only the count bounds: a full 200-event ledger window marks `events` and `planning` (older planning changes may be left out), and a graph list cut at 10 graphs, or a source that cut its own list, marks `handoffs`. Handoffs are a bounded, handoff-only ledger read per graph (the newest 10 packets, never the graph's whole history); a graph whose read failed is named in `notes` (`handoffs: N of M graphs unavailable`) and marks `handoffs` truncated, and when no graph's read answered the source is `handoffs: unavailable`, not an empty list. The repository is looked up as the caller spelled it (`Acme/Widgets` finds what the ledger stored as `Acme/Widgets`); only the session scope key is lower case. Passing `session_id` leaves that session's own rows out. It is a fail-open read that writes nothing, and the route and the tool return the same object. An anonymous caller is refused. **Opt-in:** the tool and the route are part of the sessions feature set, so with `SUBSTRATE_BRIEF_SESSIONS` off the tool answers the typed refusal `session-context.disabled` and the route answers 503 with the same `reason`, both after the usual audit, and nothing is read.
- **Long poll.** `SUBSTRATE_STEERING_WAIT_MAX_MS` (default 30000, 1000..120000) is the longest a wait is held and the default when `timeout_ms` is omitted. A `timeout_ms` above it is clamped to it rather than refused, and the answer names the wait it got: `wait_ms` in a `200 { directive, wait_ms }` and the `X-Steering-Wait-Ms` header on a `204`; only a `timeout_ms` that is not a whole number of at least 1 is a 400. The `after` cursor is the directive's place in the order directives entered the inbox (a counter owned by the inbox, assigned in restore order at boot), not a timestamp, so a clock that steps back or two directives in one millisecond never hide a queued directive; an `after` the inbox does not know (a restart forgot it) restarts from the first queued directive and the listener dedupes by id. A client that disconnects, a timeout and server shutdown each end the wait and leave no waiter, timer or listener behind.
- **Durable authority, not registry cache.** `SUBSTRATE_LIVE_SESSION_LEASE_S` defaults to 3600 seconds, bounds 60..86400. Owner-proof updates/heartbeat renew; reads, discovery, operator queue/ack and presence do not. Heartbeat every 300 seconds at default, earlier for short leases, including quiet sessions. Expiry/close are terminal; open a new identity, never reset/re-register. Historical `session.end` has no live side effect. Mandatory migration 42 runs independently of historical migration-37 selection and cache availability. Missing authority/schema refuses private access; no cache fallback.
- **Acknowledged directives after a restart.** Boot restores only the open directives. `GET /steering/directives` (status `acknowledged` or `all`) therefore also reads the session's most recent 100 acknowledged directives from `planning_steering_audit` and lists those the inbox no longer holds. The audit table records the acknowledgement, not what it did to the session, so a directive read back from it carries `checkpoint_hint: null`: a hint is named only for a move this process saw, and a hook that did not consume its hint before a restart takes no steering checkpoint for that directive. The answer carries `history` (`durable`, `memory-only` when `SUBSTRATE_PLANNING_STEERING_AUDIT_TABLE` is off so only the in-memory view exists, or `unavailable` when the table could not be read) and `history_truncated`.
- **Brief sections and identity split.** With `SUBSTRATE_BRIEF_SESSIONS` on, live public sessions/handoffs and private checked steering sections append per request, each bounded to 1200 characters, never stored in cached sections/last-good snapshots. Private steering needs `steering_session_id` and transport proof, not historical `session_id`; the latter retains worktree/self-exclusion attribution. Explicit live requests are checked even with display flags off and across full/fast/cache/snapshot/ETag branches, and rechecked after awaits before private release/304. Private denial cannot become a successful degraded/omitted section. Worktree overlap remains independent under `SUBSTRATE_WORKTREE_REGISTRY`, with existing repo/historical label, deadline and caps ([live-steering.md](live-steering.md#worktree-overlap-in-the-brief)).
- **Session boundary (phase 71).** Owner plus independent proof is required for private access; same-surface sibling without proof, foreign, unknown, expired and closed targets share REST 404 `{"status":"refused","error":{"code":"not-found"}}` without target/ownership/audit decoration. MCP carries that typed refusal in an error tool result. Trusted operators bypass owner proof only for private-read/close/queue/ack, never existence/expiry/terminal status or owner renewal/update. Correctly bound nonoperator queue/ack is 403 `operator-required`. Missing authority is 503 `session-unavailable`; required mutation audit failure is 503 `audit-unavailable`. Mandatory principal 429 precedes lookup/inference and all audits. Target preparation precedes generic audit; admitted required mutation audit precedes effect; MCP boundary-refused batches dispatch nothing. Refusal events hash targets only, and audit outage does not change 404.
- **Release and public history.** Private reads recheck SQL after awaits and before release; close/expiry then refuses private 200/204/304. SQL/inbox commits instead linearize mutations: committed issued/updated/renewed/closed/queued/acknowledged receipts stay truthful public metadata after later close/outage, not a retroactive 404 or rollback. Issuance still returns proof once to its originating creator; if concurrently closed that proof is unusable, not a promise of continued liveness. `event_stored: false` on issuance/update/close means the subsequent best-effort public note failed, not SQL failure; required generic audit still precedes mutation effects. An acknowledgement may stand while state application refuses; an already committed movement keeps applied metadata while a failed final read check withholds the hint. New live event producers exclude directions, planning hashes, proof/hash, body/count and augmented questions. Historical query authorization/rows remain unchanged, not retroactively private.

The operator guide is [live-steering.md](live-steering.md); rollback is in [rollback-compat.md](runbooks/rollback-compat.md).

## Docs reflect (phase 66)

The docs plane learns from use. One migration step, 38 `corpus_reflect` (additive, `expand`, always run), adds a nullable `superseded_by` column to `corpus_documents` and two tables to the index, and nothing reads or writes them while the flags below are off. Nothing in this section needs an LLM route: every number is a count or a pure function of counts.

- **Telemetry (`corpus_retrievals`).** With `SUBSTRATE_DOCS_TELEMETRY=on`, every `docs_search` with a question appends one row after its answer is built: the surface, `query_hash` (sha256 of the query trimmed, lower-cased and with whitespace collapsed), `query_text` (the query cut to 200 characters, or null when the redactor would change it, so a secret in a question is never stored, not even masked), the backend that answered, `returned` (at most ten `{key, score}` entries: `repo:path` for a corpus document, the source URL cut to 300 characters for any other; never a snippet or any text), the best score, the citation count, `degraded` (the plane could not answer) and `rerank` (shadow mode only). The write is fail-open and never waited for: it starts when the answer is ready, runs under a 2 s budget with at most 50 writes in flight (a slower database drops rows), and every outcome is counted (`Substrate.reflectCounters()`); a failure is logged on the first and then on every hundredth, never per search. Cached answers are recorded too: the row says what the caller was given. With the flag off nothing is written and nothing is swept.
- **Retention.** Rows older than `SUBSTRATE_DOCS_TELEMETRY_RETENTION_DAYS` (default 30, 1 to 365) are deleted oldest first in batches of 1000, up to 50 batches or 2 seconds per sweep, once at boot and then once an hour, and only while `SUBSTRATE_DOCS_TELEMETRY` is on. The sweep is never in the search path. Turning the flag off leaves the rows already written where they are until it is turned on again or an operator deletes them.
- **Feedback (`corpus_feedback`).** `POST /docs/feedback` and the `docs_feedback` tool take `{document, verdict, session_id?, note?}`: `document` is the key a corpus citation's `title` gives (`repo:path`), `verdict` is `helpful`, `unhelpful`, `stale`, `wrong` or `duplicate`, and `note` is accepted and ignored (never stored, never audited). One row per document and session, the latest verdict wins, so a repeat is harmless; the stored session is the caller's surface plus its `session_id` (or the surface alone), so one surface never overwrites another's verdict. An anonymous caller is refused `401`; a server with `SUBSTRATE_CORPUS` off answers `503 corpus.disabled`; a document that is not an active manifest row answers `404 unknown-document` and stores nothing. Admission class `write`; audited fail-open under `docs_feedback` with the document and the verdict only. The verdict counts per document are read by one grouped query, cached for 60 s, over at most 5000 documents (the most recently voted on); this process drops the cache when it stores a verdict.
- **Authority.** `scoreDocument` (`packages/corpus/src/authority.ts`) is a pure function of tier, kind, age (days since the content last reached the plane; a metadata refresh does not reset it), supersession and the feedback counts, in 0 to 1. The server computes it per cited document from an in-process index: the active manifest rows (at most 100,000, so a document past the cap is never flagged or re-ranked, never wrongly so) and `detectSupersession` over them, cached under the manifest version. The version is read at most every 30 s and the rows again only when it moved; an ingest or a reflect run on this process drops the index at once. A manifest that fails to read keeps the last index, and with none the answer is returned untouched; a lookup that has not finished in 500 ms is not waited for.
- **Supersession.** A document is superseded when a newer version of the same path exists, when it is a `milestones/` archive of a live `.planning` file, or when its own front matter carries a `superseded-by` marker (a repo-relative path of the same repository, stored in `superseded_by`; an invalid marker is dropped, never a refusal, and a changed marker is a manifest-only `update`). `corpus/plan.ts` and `corpus/ingest.ts` treat a changed marker like a changed tier or kind.
- **Re-rank (`SUBSTRATE_DOCS_RERANK`).** `off` (default, and anything unrecognised) reads nothing and the answer is byte-identical to a build without this phase. `shadow` computes the order `on` would give and records it, with the number of positions that would move, in the search's telemetry row (and a per-process counter), and leaves the answer untouched. `on` attaches `stale` (the document is superseded, or at least 3 readers called it stale and more did so than called it helpful) and `superseded_by` (the key of the replacing document, or null) to each corpus citation the manifest holds, re-orders those citations by `score × (0.5 + 0.5 × authority)` (stable: equal values keep the plane's order, and a superseded document drops below its replacement when their scores are comparable), and adds `rerank: { applied: true, moved: n }` to the answer. Citations that are not corpus documents, or whose document is not an active row, keep their place and their bytes; a degraded answer and an answer with no corpus citation are never touched. The re-rank runs after the answer cache, per call, so a stored answer never carries it.
- **Digests (`POST /corpus/reflect`).** `substrate corpus reflect` sends one digest per repository (`{repo, text, sha256}`, at most 200 per request, 20,000 characters each). The server stores each as the corpus document `(repo, ".substrate/digest.md")`, kind `digest`, tier 1, source `fs`, through the same ingest path as any document, so redaction, naming, the manifest and the plane rules all apply and the request waits behind the same single-flight lock. A scan never tombstones a digest. Each digest is judged by itself: a malformed one is reported `refused` with its reason and the rest go on. A digest that was stored (`ingested` or `updated`) is then retained in Hindsight through the governance gate as a `fact` at `repo:<repo>` scope with the deterministic subject `corpus-digest:<repo>`, whose Hindsight document id is the claim identity, so the next digest replaces it; reflect and mental models are not used. That leg is best effort: a refusal or an outage becomes the result's `reason` (`hindsight:<closed reason>`) while the plane document stays stored, and a digest the manifest already held unchanged is not retained again. Needs an authenticated surface, answers `503 corpus.disabled` while `SUBSTRATE_CORPUS` is off and `503 corpus.backend-unavailable` with no LightRAG backend; audited fail-closed under `corpus_ingest` (counts only), admission class `write`.
- **Gaps (`GET /corpus/gaps?limit=N`).** The queries whose best citation over the last 30 days was none or below `SUBSTRATE_DOCS_GAP_SCORE` (default 0.2), aggregated by query hash: `searches`, the most recent redacted `text` (null when none was kept), `lastSeen` and `best`, most searched first, at most `limit` (default 50, at most 200; a value that is not a whole number is 50), with `truncated` when more exist. A search the plane could not answer is not a gap. Read-only, audited fail-open under `corpus_status`, admission class `read`; it answers an empty list until telemetry has recorded searches, and also answers `503 corpus.disabled` while `SUBSTRATE_CORPUS` is off.

| Flag | Default | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| `SUBSTRATE_DOCS_TELEMETRY` | `off` | `on` records a retrieval row per search and runs the retention sweep |
| `SUBSTRATE_DOCS_TELEMETRY_RETENTION_DAYS` | `30` | 1 to 365; rows older than this are swept |
| `SUBSTRATE_DOCS_RERANK` | `off` | `off`, `shadow` or `on`; anything else is `off` |
| `SUBSTRATE_DOCS_GAP_SCORE` | `0.2` | a number in 0 to 1; a query whose best citation scores below it is a gap |

## Corpus proofs (phase 69)

The manifest has a Merkle root: an RFC 6962 tree, built with the ledger package's own functions, over the active rows' `(repo, path, sha256)` leaves sorted by `(repo, path)`. It is computed from `corpus_documents` on the server (one read of at most 100,000 active rows) and cached per manifest version in process memory; nothing new is stored in Postgres or Dragonfly, and no migration is added.

- **Ledger.** After an ingest that moved the manifest has settled, `substrate-mcp` writes one `note` on surface `substrate`, session `corpus-root`, `payload.action: "docs.corpus.root"` with `root`, `count` and `manifest_version`, at most one per five seconds and never in the request's path. The hourly anchor covers it like any other event; the anchor job, its funded key and its timer stay operator-gated and the memo format is unchanged. The action is reserved (`RESERVED_NOTE_ACTIONS`).
- **Reads.** `GET /corpus/root`, `GET /corpus/proof` and `GET /corpus/anchor` (bearer, audited under `corpus_status`) and `substrate corpus prove|verify`. `docs_search` carries `corpus_root` and `corpus_count` from the cached root. All of it answers `503 corpus.disabled`, or leaves the reply unchanged, while `SUBSTRATE_CORPUS` is off.
- **Details.** The leaf encoding, the empty-manifest root, the anchor statuses, what a root does not cover and how a third party verifies are in `docs/corpus-proofs.md`.

## Fail-open budget (unchanged)

A substrate outage never blocks a prompt. `fetchBrief` returns `""` within 1.5 s. Every cache miss and every Dragonfly, Hindsight or LightRAG error falls through to the next source or to an empty result with a logged reason. Writes to Greptime and Timescale are the only calls allowed to fail closed, and only for the caller that asked for durability.
